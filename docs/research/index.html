Research

My research aims to make the hospital a place where people with opioid use disorder (OUD) receive effective treatment and stay connected to care after they leave. Fentanyl has changed what withdrawal looks like, how patients respond to standard medication starts, and how often they leave the hospital before treatment is complete. I use real-world clinical data, mixed methods, and implementation science to understand those gaps and to test practical, systems-level fixes.

Current focus

Withdrawal management in the fentanyl era. Opioid withdrawal is often undertreated in the hospital, and it is a common reason patients leave before treatment is complete. I am interested in how improvements to EHR-based clinical decision support could strengthen opioid withdrawal management, and I contributed to a 2026 consensus meeting on how opioid withdrawal should be measured.

Methadone and buprenorphine in the hospital. I study how methadone dosing during hospitalization relates to patient-directed discharge and readmission, and how buprenorphine and methadone initiation have changed for patients using fentanyl.

Patient-directed discharge. Leaving the hospital before treatment is complete is common among people with OUD and is associated with serious harm. My work examines its drivers and the hospital practices that can reduce it.

Data systems and policy. Hospitals often cannot confirm a patient’s methadone dose from their opioid treatment program. I have worked on integrating opioid treatment program data into Maryland’s health information exchange (CRISP), presented this work to the Maryland Health Services Cost Review Commission, and co-led a national workshop on methadone dose confirmation under federal privacy rules (42 CFR Part 2).
